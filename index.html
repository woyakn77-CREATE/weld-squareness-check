How cross-corner measuring works

The principle

A rectangle has two equal diagonals. When a frame is out of square it leans into a parallelogram: one diagonal gets longer and the other gets shorter by about the same amount. Comparing the two diagonals finds the error without a square, protractor, or angle gauge.

The longer diagonal runs between the two corners that opened past 90°. The shorter one runs between the corners that closed under 90°.

Check the sides first. Equal diagonals prove square only when opposite sides are equal. A frame with one short side can have matching diagonals and still be out of square, so confirm both lengths and both widths before trusting the diagonals.

How to measure

  1. Lay the frame on a flat table or fixture. Diagonals do not catch twist, so the part needs to sit flat first.
  2. Check that the two lengths match and the two widths match.
  3. Pick the same reference point at all four corners: outside corners, or a center punch mark set the same distance in from each corner.
  4. Hook the tape on corner 1 and read at corner 3. This is diagonal A.
  5. Hook the tape on corner 2 and read at corner 4. This is diagonal B.
  6. Enter each diagonal in the Measured field. Both readings in the green band: the frame is square within tolerance.
  7. Either reading in the red zone: hold the part and flag it for quality review before any rework.

Shop tips

  • Tack, measure, then weld. Correcting a tacked frame takes a clamp; correcting a finished weldment takes heat or a cut.
  • To correct, pull or clamp across the long diagonal. That shortens it and lengthens the other one.
  • Weld shrinkage pulls toward the weld. Alternate welds across opposite corners and re-check the diagonals between passes.
  • Use one tape for both readings and pull it with the same tension each time.
